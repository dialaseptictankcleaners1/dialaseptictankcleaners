1. The Numbers Behind the Smell

Hyderabad's urban area produces roughly 1,950 million litres of sewage every day: about 1,650 MLD inside limits and 300 MLD in the ORR belt. The city has been adding treatment plants fast, but capacity has had to chase demand.

Sewage Metric Capacity / Generation (MLD) Coverage Context
Generated daily 1,950 MLD Total urban area (ORR Growth Corridors)
Capacity, 2021 772 MLD Treated approx 46% of sewage generated
Capacity, 2024 1,338 MLD Progressive expansion across STPs
Planned capacity 1,791 MLD Upcoming infrastructure target
Sources & Regulatory Context: HMWSSB figures reported by Siasat and NBM&CW. Plans and timelines have shifted over the years; check current status with HMWSSB. In 2021, the 772 MLD of capacity treated about 46% of the sewage generated in limits.

Treatment plants only help where sewage reaches them. A Telangana government compliance report to the National Green Tribunal (NGT in OA No. 606 of 2018) noted that peripheral areas, but within the ORR have only partial sewerage. In practice, many growth corridors — including Madhapur, Gachibowli, Tellapur, Kokapet, and Kollur — still depend on septic tanks, soak pits, and private drainage that property owners and RWAs must maintain.

2. Why a Metro Makes Everything Worse

Urban growth multiplies the pressure on underground drainage and wastewater systems. In Hyderabad's high-growth corridors, four factors trigger rapid blockages:

🏢 Density

High-rises and gated communities put hundreds of flats on one tank or STP, loading sludge exponentially faster.

🌧️ Monsoon surges

Rain-fed inflow overloads drains and pushes stormwater silt and sludge backward into connecting lines.

🍳 Kitchen grease (FOG)

Restaurants, canteens, and residential towers cool fats, oils, and grease into solid plugs that choke mainlines.

🚗 Basements & cellars

Low-lying sump pits and drain pipes in B1/B2/B3 parking back up first whenever pressure rises.

⚠️
Critical Warning: A full septic tank is not a "later" problem. It becomes a same-night emergency the moment it overflows.

3. Is Your System Already Asking for Help?

Drainage systems give clear physical warning signals days before a catastrophic backflow. Watch out for these symptoms:

  • Slow drains in more than one bathroom or kitchen.
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or floor traps.
  • Foul odour near the tank cover, garden or parking area.
  • Wet, spongy patches or unusually green grass over the tank.
  • Backflow in the lowest drain when the upper floors use water.
🚨
Inspection Rule: Any one of these is a signal. Two or more means it is time to book a cleaning now rather than after an overflow.

5. How Often Should You Clean?

Sludge accumulation is silent. Regular preventive scheduling keeps bacteria active and stops solids from spilling into soak wells or municipal mains:

Property Type General Guideline* Key Maintenance Factor
Independent house / villa Every 2–3 years Based on tank volume and household head count
Apartments & gated societies Yearly inspection Clean as sludge level demands (quarterly or biannual pump-outs)
Restaurants & kitchens (grease trap) Monthly Common for busy kitchens to prevent solid grease blockages
Basements & sump pits Before monsoon & after backflow Ensure submersible pumps and holding pits have 100% capacity

*General industry guidance, not a legal standard. Your tank size and usage decide the real interval; the crew can advise after inspection.
