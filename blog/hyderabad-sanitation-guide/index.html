Hyderabad Flushes 1,950 Million Litres a Day. Where Does It All Go?

✍️ By Dial-a-Septic Tank Cleaners • 📅 October 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read • 📍 Greater Hyderabad Region

Every day, we flush toilets, take showers, cook food, and wash dishes. All that dirty water immediately disappears down the drain. But in a fast-growing city like Hyderabad, handling this huge amount of wastewater is a massive operation. When any pipe gets choked, a septic tank fills up, or a sewer backs up, it quickly turns into a terrible day for a family, an apartment society, or a restaurant.

1. The Real Numbers Behind Hyderabad's Sewage

Hyderabad's urban area produces around 1,950 million litres of sewage every single day (MLD). About 1,650 MLD comes from inside the main municipal limits, and another 300 MLD comes from areas around the Outer Ring Road (ORR). The government has been building new Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs) quickly, but the city is expanding even faster.

Sewage Metric Capacity / Generation (MLD) What It Means
Generated daily 1,950 MLD Total wastewater produced daily across Hyderabad and the ORR belt
Capacity in 2021 772 MLD Treated only about 46% of sewage produced in city limits
Capacity in 2024 1,338 MLD Increased capacity after several new STPs opened
Planned capacity 1,791 MLD Target capacity for upcoming government plants
Where These Numbers Come From: Official HMWSSB figures reported by Siasat and NBM&CW. While new plants have been built over the years, in 2021 only about 46% of the city's sewage could be treated in plants.

Remember, treatment plants only help if sewage lines actually connect to your building. Official government compliance reports submitted to the National Green Tribunal (NGT OA No. 606 of 2018) noted that outer areas along the ORR have only partial sewer connections. That means popular growth areas — such as Madhapur, Gachibowli, Tellapur, Kokapet, Kondapur, and Kollur — still depend heavily on private septic tanks, soak pits, and building drains that property owners and apartment committees must clean regularly.

2. Why City Life Makes Drainage Problems Worse

In a crowded metro city, underground pipes face heavy daily stress. Here are 4 main reasons why drains choke so easily in Hyderabad:

🏢 1. High Density Living

Modern high-rises and gated communities place hundreds of families on a single septic tank or STP. Sludge builds up much faster than in independent villas.

🌧️ 2. Monsoon Surges

Heavy rains flood stormwater drains. Mud and street silt wash backward into building sewer lines, causing sudden blockages.

🍳 3. Kitchen Oil & Grease (FOG)

Restaurants, canteens, and home kitchens pour cooking oil, ghee, and fats down the sink. When hot grease cools in the pipes, it solidifies into hard wax-like plugs that block water completely.

🚗 4. Basements & Underground Parking

Underground parking levels (B1, B2, B3) sit at the lowest point. When mainline pipes get choked, sewage water pushes back into cellar floor traps first.

⚠️
Urgent Warning: A full septic tank will not wait. The moment it overflows, it turns into an emergency that same night!

3. 5 Warning Signs That Your System Needs Immediate Cleaning

Underground drainage systems almost always give warning signals days before dirty water overflows into your home. Look out for these 5 common signs:

  • Slow Draining Water: Water drains slowly in more than one bathroom or kitchen sink.
  • Gurgling Bubbles: Gurgling or bubbling sounds coming from toilet bowls or floor traps.
  • Bad Smells: Foul, rotten odor near the septic tank cover, garden, or parking lot.
  • Soggy Ground: Wet, spongy soil or unusually dark green grass growing right over your septic tank.
  • Dirty Backflow: Dirty water pushing back up in ground floor or basement drains when upper floors use water.
🚨
Golden Rule: Even one of these is a warning sign. Two or more means it is time to book a cleaning tanker right now before an overflow ruins your home!

5. How Often Should You Clean Your Septic Tank?

Solid sludge builds up slowly at the bottom of your septic tank. Cleaning your tank on time keeps healthy bacteria working and prevents solid waste from choking your soak pit or drainage lines:

Type of Property How Often to Clean* Why This Matters
Independent house / villa Every 2 to 3 years Depends on your tank capacity and family size
Apartments & gated societies Inspect every year Clean whenever sludge levels rise (usually every 6 to 12 months)
Restaurants & food kitchens Every month Busy kitchens produce heavy oil and grease that can choke mainlines quickly
Basements & sump pits Before monsoon & after any backflow Ensures sump pumps and drainage pits have 100% free capacity for rains

*General industry guidance. Your tank size and daily water usage decide the real cleaning interval. Our field team can inspect and guide you on-site.
